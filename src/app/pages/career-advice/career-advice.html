Career Advice

Tips to grow your developer career

Bite-sized advice to help you level up, one idea at a time

Build in public

Share what you're learning and building online, even if it's small. Consistency compounds — a year of small posts builds more credibility than one perfect portfolio piece.

Master the fundamentals first

Frameworks change every few years, but a solid grasp of HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and how the web works will always transfer. Don't skip the basics to chase the newest tool.

Read other people's code

Reviewing open-source projects and your teammates' pull requests teaches you patterns and pitfalls that tutorials never will. Reading code is as important as writing it.

Don't chase every new framework

Depth beats breadth. Employers value someone who can debug and scale a real application over someone who has tried ten frameworks superficially.

Ask questions early, not after hours of struggle

There's a difference between productive struggle and wasted time. If you've been stuck for 30+ minutes, ask — good engineers know when to seek help.

Write code for humans, not just computers

Clear naming, small functions, and helpful comments matter more than clever one-liners. You'll spend more time reading code than writing it — including your own, six months later.

Learn to estimate and communicate delays

Being upfront about a slipping deadline builds more trust than silently struggling and delivering late with no warning. Managers can plan around bad news, not surprises.

Your network matters as much as your skills

Most opportunities come through people, not job boards. Stay in touch with former colleagues, contribute to communities, and don't underestimate a simple coffee chat.
